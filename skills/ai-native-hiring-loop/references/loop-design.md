# Designing the loop

## Outcomes before competencies

Write 2 to 4 outcomes for the first year first, each with a measure and a month. Every competency must serve at least one, and the design check blocks one that serves none. If an outcome has no competency, the loop is not testing for it.

## Anchors

Anchors describe what the interviewer sees or reads, not a trait. "States a definition of good with a target and a window" is an anchor. "Strategic" is not. A 3 is the bar. A 4 is "all of 3, and" something specific.

## Work samples

A work sample is the only place the three kinds of AI judgment count toward coverage. Design it so that a candidate who uses a model for everything scores lower than one who chooses:

- Give real-shaped material (anonymised or invented), a time box and one decision to make.
- Allow AI tools and ask the candidate to say where they used them. Score the judgment, not whether they used a tool.
- Include at least one step a model should not do (arithmetic, tax, legal or payment wording, an irreversible send).
- Ask for eval cases with a pass rule, and a cost per task stated as tokens per task and price per token.
- Two interviewers score it independently against the criteria anchors.

## Structured interview questions

- Write every question down. Every candidate gets every question, in the same order.
- Mix behavioural ("tell me about a time") and situational ("what would you do if") questions.
- Each question maps to one competency and is scored against that competency's anchors.
- Follow-ups are written too, so probing is the same for everyone.

## Banned criteria

The design check blocks terms that invite bias or measure something other than the job, including "culture fit", "gut feel", "overqualified", "digital native", "young", "energetic", "native speaker", "pedigree" and "top school". Replace them with the behaviour you mean: "brings design and engineering into framing" instead of "culture fit".

## Calibration

Keep the calibration steps in the template unless the user has a reason to change them. The two that matter most: every score is recorded, with evidence, before the debrief starts; and scores do not change in the debrief.
